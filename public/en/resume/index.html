About me

I turn emerging technology into real products.

I started programming at 13. In 2015, still a teenager, I started building Glassear because I wanted AR glasses I couldn't buy. Glassear needed hardware, so I learned hardware; it needed computer vision, so I learned computer vision. Later came backend, infrastructure and AI. The technologies changed; the habit didn't: pick a hard problem, build it, break it, understand why, and build it again until it works outside a demo.

How I think

AI should do things, not just talk about them.

It gets interesting when a model can use real tools, change a real scene or call a real API, with deterministic checks around it deciding what actually happens.

The prototype is where the real work starts.

Making something work once is a demo. Making it work on an ordinary Tuesday, for someone else, with bad Wi-Fi, is a product.

I like owning the whole problem.

Frontend, backend, hardware, AI or infrastructure are implementation choices. If it's part of the product, it's part of the problem.

Emerging tech gets interesting when it stops feeling emerging.

AR and AI don't matter because they're new. They matter the day a musician reads a score hands-free, or a team stops doing something by hand.
